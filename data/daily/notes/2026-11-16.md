## Crypto & Digital Assets

**1. OpenAI's agent checkout launched on card networks, with no crypto or stablecoins** — MAJOR (also in AI)
- **What happened:** OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet on Nov 13. It was built with American Express, Chase, Mastercard and Visa, and starts in "dots" agents for US ChatGPT Pro subscribers. Several outlets confirm it cannot pay with crypto or stablecoins.
- **Why it matters:** The largest consumer AI agent platform chose card rails for its first agent payments. That weakens the thesis that agent commerce defaults to crypto rails, at least for consumers.
- **Watch:** Whether stablecoins get added. Visa has said it wants agents paying with cards or stablecoins. Also watch for crypto openings in business-to-business or machine-to-machine payments.
- **Status:** Confirmed. The launch is from OpenAI's own announcement; the "no crypto" detail comes from secondary outlets.
- **Sources:**

**2. Kraken parent Payward Q3: profit down sharply, less dependent on trading fees** — MODERATE
- **What happened:**
  - Adjusted EBITDA was $60M, down 67% from a year earlier (per Bloomberg) but up from $23M in Q2.
  - Total platform margin, a new headline metric replacing adjusted revenue, was $487M, down 12% year on year and up 12% on Q2.
  - Trading volume fell 30% to $353B.
  - Income from client balances and subscriptions/services rose to 52% of the total, from 46% a year earlier.
  - Payward plans US prediction markets and a personal-finance AI agent.
- **Why it matters:** Exchanges are shifting to recurring revenue that holds up in a down market. That changes how to value exchange and broker businesses.
- **Watch:** Q4 volumes during the selloff, and the timing of the prediction-markets launch.
- **Status:** Confirmed through Bloomberg and trade press. I did not open the shareholder letter itself.

## Energy & Grid

**1. PJM asked FERC to require large data centers to stay online through grid disturbances** — MAJOR
- **What happened:**
  - PJM, the largest US grid operator, filed the proposal Nov 16.
  - It creates a new "Large Computational Load" category for sites of 50 MW or more.
  - Requirements would take effect Jan 16, 2027. Existing sites get one year to comply.
  - Trigger: on July 22, nearly 3.9 GW of data centers suddenly switched to on-site power during a routine grid disturbance, the largest such event in PJM's history.
  - PJM serves about 10 GW of these loads today and forecasts up to 30 GW more by 2030.
- **Why it matters:** Grid connection now comes with technical performance requirements. That creates a retrofit market across existing data centers for power electronics, UPS controls, batteries and grid-stabilizing equipment.
- **Watch:** FERC comment deadlines, ramp-rate rules PJM says are coming next, and whether other grid operators copy it.
- **Status:** Confirmed, primary source.
- **Source:** PJM Inside Lines: https://insidelines.pjm.com/pjm-proposes-new-rules-to-protect-grid-reliability-as-data-center-demand-grows/

**2. Local voters are pushing back on data centers** — MODERATE
- **What happened:** Time (Nov 16) reports that in 40 of 41 local ballot measures in this month's elections, voters chose to limit or block data center development. The article covers a new study of hyperscale data center electricity use.
- **Why it matters:** Permits and community approval are becoming a bottleneck alongside grid capacity, which helps on-site generation and smaller distributed sites.
- **Status:** Single source for the ballot figure.
- **Source:** TIME: https://time.com/article/2026/11/16/ai-data-centers-electricity-carbon-emissions-study/
