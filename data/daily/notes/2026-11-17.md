## Headline (cross-sector): The US now requires export licenses for overseas compute that serves Chinese companies

**AI Systems / Deep Tech / Energy & Grid / Crypto — MAJOR — Confirmed (primary source)**

- **What happened:** On Monday, Nov 16, the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security issued an interim final rule titled "Addressing Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors" (RIN 0694-AK50). I read the 132-page text. It does four things:
  - It requires a license to ship advanced chips to any data center, anywhere in the world, that will give companies headquartered in China, Macau or other countries of concern access to more than 12,000,000 "aggregate TPP" of compute. That is roughly 760 H100s, according to a third-party estimate that matches my own arithmetic.
  - The threshold applies across "linked data centers," which explicitly **includes digitally-linked infrastructure**, not just data centers that are physically connected. AI recommendation engines used by retailers and social media are exempt.
  - It requires a new license to ship advanced chips to **Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil**. Companies headquartered in the US are exempt if they meet reporting requirements.
  - It tightens the rules for chip foundries and packagers, and formally rescinds the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule.
- **Timing:** The rule takes effect when it is published in the Federal Register, with 60 days for comments.
- **Context:** Commerce said the rule was "written by and at the direction of" Treasury and the National Security Council. Bloomberg quoted a former Commerce official asking which agency will actually enforce it. The rule follows Jensen Huang's Nov 12 resignation as Nvidia CEO amid an espionage scandal. Singapore said it is talking to US officials about "trusted access" to chips.
- **Market reaction Monday (Reuters):** Chip stocks fell 4.6% (Philadelphia Semiconductor Index), the Nasdaq fell 2.3% and is now in a correction, Nvidia fell 4.2%, CoreWeave fell 6.2% and Nebius fell 7.5%.
- **Why it matters:** Access to compute is now licensed, and data centers must know their customers and their ultimate owners. The exemption for US-headquartered companies favors US hyperscalers over local or non-US cloud providers in fast-growing data center markets like Johor (Malaysia), Singapore and Brazil. Chinese AI labs lose a key workaround: a think tank (Institute for Progress) estimated that about one-third of China's effective AI compute would come from remote access by end-2027. That figure comes via a secondary write-up.
- **My interpretation, not legal advice:** The "digitally-linked infrastructure" wording could reach decentralized GPU networks that combine many machines into one pool and have customers owned from China. That would directly affect M31's decentralized compute thesis.
- **What to watch:**
  - The Federal Register publication date and which agency takes the lead on enforcement.
  - Nvidia's Q3 results today after the close, its first under interim CEO Colette Kress. Reuters says analysts expect about $109.1B in revenue.
  - Trump's trip to China and expected meeting with Xi. On Monday he also said he would welcome Huang returning as Nvidia CEO.
