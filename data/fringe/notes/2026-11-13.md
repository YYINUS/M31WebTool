### 1. vgrav — PENDING
- **Field:** MOND / modified gravity
- **Source and date:** software and reproducibility package on Zenodo, version 2.0.0, published Nov 15, 2026
- **Claim:** A code package fits dark matter halos and six modified-gravity models to both the Milky Way's rotation curve (radial) and its vertical gravity. The accompanying paper isn't out yet, so we don't know the results.
- **Authors:** Robert Monjo (CUNEF University; Universidad de Chile). The paper is listed as R. Monjo & A. E. Sasi, 2026, "in preparation." Sasi's affiliation is not in the source.
- **Link:** https://zenodo.org/records/24250765 · DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24250765
- **Evidence stage:** 0. Only the code and the method description exist; no results seen.
- **Impact if true:** MEDIUM. Requiring one model to fit both the radial and the vertical gravity is a harder test than fitting a rotation curve alone.
- **What it shows:** The planned method.
  - Models: NFW and Einasto halos, QUMOND, STVG, Verlinde's emergent gravity, f(R), refracted gravity and hyperconical modified gravity (HMG).
  - Data: 152 Milky Way rotation-curve points that don't depend on a model, plus 44 vertical-potential measurements from Wang et al. (2026).
  - Statistics: four sets of 100 random draws of the visible (baryonic) mass, compared with the Bayesian information criterion (BIC). QUMOND, STVG and refracted gravity are solved with their full field equations.
- **Still unknown:** which model wins; how much the baryonic-mass assumptions drive the answer; whether either author developed one of the models tested (not stated in the source).
- **More convincing if:** the paper is shared publicly with the full ranking, the result holds across the four baryonic sets, and someone outside the group reruns the code.
- **Would prove it wrong:** If HMG or another niche model "wins" only because of a particular baryonic set, that is a fragile result. If MOND-type models fit the radial data but fail the vertical data, that counts against them.
- **Who to follow:** Robert Monjo (lead author and code maintainer).
- **What to do:** Wait for the paper. Trigger: it appears on arXiv with a BIC table. Then have a reviewer rerun the package (it is 29.2 MB) before any contact.
- **Compare with:** recent wide-binary tests reporting no MOND signal:
  - "A Quality Framework for Testing Gravity with Wide Binaries: No Evidence for MOND": https://arxiv.org/html/2602.24035
  - "No Gravitational Anomaly in Wide Binaries from Forward Modeling of 3D Orbits": https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11015
  - High-precision radial velocities for C3PO wide binaries: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.19652

### 2. CCC+TL — PENDING
- **Field:** modified cosmology (no dark matter or dark energy)
- **Source and date:** University of Ottawa press release, republished by SciTechDaily on Nov 13, 2026
- **Claim:** A new galaxy study is said to support CCC+TL, a model in which neither dark matter nor dark energy is needed and the universe is 26.7 billion years old.
- **Authors:** Rajendra Gupta (adjunct professor of physics, University of Ottawa) and a postdoctoral researcher whose name was cut off in what I saw.
- **Link:** https://scitechdaily.com/a-universe-without-dark-matter-radical-new-model-challenges-modern-cosmology/ (the full page wouldn't load; details come from the search excerpt). Paper title, venue and DOI not seen.
- **Evidence stage:** 0. Only the press release was seen.
- **Impact if true:** HIGH. But the model's age for the universe is about twice the standard value.
- **Shows / unknown:** The release says the galaxy study is one of a series of tests of CCC+TL run in 2025–26. What was tested and how well it fits are unknown.
- **More convincing if:** the model matches the cosmic microwave background power spectrum, element abundances from the Big Bang and the ages of the oldest stars at the same time, not one dataset at a time.
- **Would prove it wrong:** a clear conflict with the microwave background or with stellar ages. A reader comment on the article says galaxy sizes conflict with the model, but that is not a published critique.
- **Who to follow:** Rajendra Gupta.
- **What to do:** Ignore unless the underlying paper turns out to be peer-reviewed and tests the microwave background.
- **Compare with:** Planck 2018 cosmological parameters, which give an age of about 13.8 billion years: https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.06209

### 3. Euclid lenses — BENCHMARK
- **Field:** gravitational lensing; a constraint on modified gravity
- **Source and date:** University of British Columbia (UBC) news story, published by Phys.org on Nov 15, 2026
- **Claim:** Euclid has found about 15,000 candidate strong gravitational lenses, more than all earlier collections combined. They come as a single, uniformly analysed catalogue.
- **Who:** UBC researchers, including a scientist named Srivastava (first name not in what I saw).
- **Link:** https://phys.org/news/2026-11-dark-matterhunting-satellite-euclid-reveals.html (the page wouldn't load; details come from the search excerpt). Catalogue paper not identified.
- **Evidence stage:** 2. These are observed candidates, not yet confirmed lenses.
- **Why it matters:** Any MOND-type theory has to explain lensing without dark matter. A large catalogue makes it much harder to pick only the systems that suit a theory.
- **What to do:** Find the catalogue paper next week. Rule: any MOND or emergent-gravity proposal we consider must say how it handles strong lensing statistics.

### 4. Dark energy sign flip — PENDING
- **Field:** vacuum and dark-energy physics
- **Source and date:** ScienceDaily, Nov 13, 2026
- **Claim:** A reading of DESI galaxy-survey data allows two cosmic histories. In one, the effective dark energy had negative density in the distant past. Which history applies depends on today's matter density.
- **Authors:** not in the source excerpt.
- **Link:** https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/11/261111005901.htm. Paper not seen.
- **Evidence stage:** 0. Only the press release was seen.
- **Impact if true:** HIGH as context. A dark energy that changes over time would change the mainstream picture that vacuum-energy claims are judged against.
- **Caveat:** The release itself says the findings "do not yet establish that dark energy changes over time."
- **What to do:** Wait. Trigger: the paper is identified and DESI's own collaboration analyses support a dark energy that changes over time. Until then, keep a constant dark energy (Λ) as the benchmark for vacuum claims.

### 5. Galileo observatory — WATCH
- **Field:** UAP
- **Source and date:** IBTimes UK news report, Nov 13, 2026
- **Claim:** Avi Loeb's Galileo Project plans a roughly $1.5M observatory in Ithaca, New York. Radar would find targets and point a 0.7 m PlaneWave CDK700 telescope at them, with AI support and spectra. Readings would be checked against known flight activity.
- **People:** Avi Loeb (Harvard; described as chair of the UAP Science Advisory Council). Backer: entrepreneur Adam Weitsman. The site is a property once owned by Carl Sagan.
- **Link:** https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/avi-loeb-ufo-telescope-project-ithaca-1832101. The headline's "prove UFOs are foreign black projects" framing is not supported by the article, which treats that only as a hypothesis to investigate.
- **Evidence stage:** 0. This is a plan.
- **Impact if true:** MEDIUM. It would be the project's fourth observatory and its first to combine continuous radar with a large optical telescope. Calibrated, multi-sensor data is what the UAP field lacks.
- **Timeline:** A Nov 5 announcement said work would start this month and finish in about a year. No discoveries have been claimed.
- **Would make it convincing:** published calibration, a false-positive rate measured against known aircraft and satellites, and open data.
- **What to do:** Watch. Trigger: a first commissioning or instrument paper with radar–optical cross-matching. Only then consider supporting an independent data audit.

### 6. Out-of-body EEG — CONTEXT
- **Field:** consciousness science (also bears on psi)
- **Source and date:** VICE, Nov 14, 2026. It follows a ScienceAlert story from Nov 12 about a paper published earlier in 2026. The exact publication date is unconfirmed, but it is before the window.
- **Claim:** "Unusual bodily experiences," including out-of-body experiences, come with brain activity that mixes features of sleep and waking.
- **Authors:** Teresa Campillo-Ferrer (Radboud University) and colleagues, in *Consciousness and Cognition*. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.concog.2026.104002
- **Evidence stage:** 3, based on the news coverage. Brain recordings during reported experiences were compared with the participants' own normal sleep and meditation recordings. No independent replication or preregistration was reported.
- **What it shows:**
  - 35 participants wore EEG caps; 20 of them reported 36 experiences; 10 involved being outside or above the body.
  - Participants marked each experience with a set left-right eye-movement signal.
  - Slow waves (delta, theta) dropped and fast waves (beta, gamma) rose, mainly over the temporal lobes.
- **Unknown:** whether the whole brain was in an in-between state or different regions were asleep and awake at once.
- **Why it matters for us:** It offers an ordinary brain mechanism that any claim of accurate perception during out-of-body experiences has to rule out.
- **Would weaken it:** the same EEG pattern showing up equally often without any reported experience.
- **What to do:** Read the paper. File it as the comparison point for any out-of-body-experience proposal. A bioRxiv preprint from July 2026 on brain-signal complexity during these experiences turned up in search but I didn't open it.
