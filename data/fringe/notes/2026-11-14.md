### 1. UAP–dark matter · **WATCH**
- **Field / source:** UAP, speculative particle physics · arXiv preprint (hep-ph), 2026-11-17
- **Claim:** One kind of dark matter (axion quark nuggets, AQN) could explain several reported UAP features: high speed, extreme acceleration, no leftover material, and no sonic boom despite supersonic motion.
- **Author:** Ariel Zhitnitsky (affiliation not in source). He developed the AQN model and has used it before to explain other anomalies, such as the ANITA events (arXiv:2105.01668).
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23373
- **Evidence stage: 1.** It's a theoretical argument. The abstract cites no UAP dataset or comparison group.
- **Impact if true:** HIGH. It would link UAP to dark matter physics. The author says he uses the same parameters as his earlier AQN work, which limits how much the model can be tuned.
- **What it shows:** It argues that some UAP belong with sky-quakes, ball lightning and other unexplained transient events, all driven by the AQN's antiquark core.
- **Still unknown:**
  - Which specific UAP events are modelled, if any.
  - Whether it makes numerical predictions for brightness, sound or radiation.
  - How it fits with existing AQN constraints.
- **Would make it more convincing:** A testable prediction, for example a radiation or acoustic signature, compared against UAP records that include sensor data (Galileo Project, or AARO cases with official analysis).
- **Would prove it wrong:** Well-instrumented supersonic UAP with no matching signature, or the AQN parameter range being ruled out by other dark matter searches.
- **Critiques:** I found none.
- **Who to follow:** Zhitnitsky (author).
- **What to do / trigger:** Nothing this week. Reconsider if a follow-up compares the model with a sensor-instrumented UAP dataset.
- **Compare with:** Galileo Project's first triangulation results (Avi Loeb's blog, about 8 weeks old): https://avi-loeb.medium.com/first-triangulation-results-in-search-for-uap-by-the-galileo-project-observatories-eacc4798a538

### 2. Casimir puzzle · **BENCHMARK** (and read first)
- **Field / source:** Zero-point and vacuum physics · arXiv preprint (quant-ph), 2026-11-19
- **Claim:** A precise measurement of the magnetic field reflected from a copper plate disagrees with both standard ways of modelling how metals respond (the "Drude" and "plasma" models). The phase of the reflected field, measured here for the first time, disagrees qualitatively with the Drude model.
- **Authors:** M. Dhital, G. L. Klimchitskaya, V. M. Mostepanenko, U. Mohideen (affiliations not seen in what I read).
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25856
- **Background:** The "Casimir puzzle" is that precise Casimir force measurements fit the plasma model and not the Drude model, even though the Drude model is the physically expected one. This experiment probes the specific kind of field (s-polarised evanescent waves) at the centre of that disagreement.
- **Evidence stage: 2.** It's one team's measurement compared with theory. I don't know of an independent repeat, and the full paper wasn't read.
- **Impact if true:** MEDIUM. It means the standard theory of the vacuum force near metals is itself unresolved. Any device claiming to extract zero-point energy has to be judged against a baseline that is not fully settled.
- **What it shows:** According to the paper, the plasma model disagrees with this data even more strongly than the Drude model does, so neither explains it.
- **Still unknown:** How large the discrepancy is in standard-deviation terms, and the error budget. I saw only excerpts.
- **Would make it more convincing:** An independent group repeating the magnitude and phase measurement.
- **Would prove it wrong:** An independent measurement that agrees with the Drude model.
- **Who to follow:** Umar Mohideen (experiment); Klimchitskaya and Mostepanenko (theory).
- **What to do:** Read it. Use it as the reference standard for any zero-point energy proposal. Decision rule: if an independent lab offers to repeat it, scoping a replication would be in remit.
- **Compare with:** The same group's earlier paper "Casimir effect invalidates the Drude model for TE evanescent waves", https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.14026

### 3. Ionosphere SETI · **WATCH**
- **Field / source:** Technosignatures · arXiv preprint (astro-ph.IM), 2026-11-19
- **Claim:** A planet's ionosphere blocks radio emission below a certain frequency. That cutoff should rise and fall as the planet orbits its star, which could make it a signature of a technological civilisation's broadband radio leakage. It would also be a new way to study exoplanet ionospheres.
- **Author:** Michael A. Garrett (University of Manchester; Leiden Observatory and University of Malta are also listed).
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26025
- **Evidence stage: 1.** It's a model with no observations.
- **Impact if true:** MEDIUM. It gives a physical check that separates a planet's own radio leakage from local interference on Earth.
- **What it shows:** Several effects (radio waves travelling at an angle, magnetic fields, absorption) smooth the cutoff into a gradual escape function. Time delays in the atmosphere's response shift its timing a little, but the rise and fall survives.
- **Still unknown:** Whether current instruments could detect it. The author calls it "observationally challenging."
- **Would make it more convincing:** Forecasts of whether instruments such as LOFAR or SKA-Low could detect it for real target lists.
- **Would prove it wrong (as a useful test):** A finding that the cutoff falls below the frequency limit where ground-based radio telescopes can see through Earth's own ionosphere, for all realistic targets.
- **Who to follow:** Garrett.
- **Trigger:** A detectability forecast or a pilot analysis on low-frequency radio data.
- **Compare with:** Garrett's earlier framework for broadband radio leakage, MNRAS 549(3) stag991: https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/549/3/stag991/8697603

### 4. Workspace test · **ACT** (read)
- **Field / source:** Consciousness science · arXiv preprint (q-bio.NC), 2026-11-19
- **Background:** Global Workspace Theory says information becomes conscious when it is broadcast to the brain's specialised processes, which then use it.
- **Claim:** A causal test can show whether a network's "workspace" really does the computing, or just passes along answers computed elsewhere. Task accuracy and ablation tests (switching parts off) cannot tell these apart.
- **Authors:** Diaoxuan He, Linqian Wu (affiliations not in source).
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26018
- **Evidence stage: 3.** The test was checked against networks deliberately built either to relay or to recombine information, a known-answer control. According to the abstract, it identified the computation correctly in every case. No other team has repeated it.
- **Impact if true:** MEDIUM. It turns a central claim of the theory into a property you can test in trained models. That matters for claims about consciousness in AI.
- **Still unknown:** How well it works on large pretrained models. The abstract mentions only one known interface in a pretrained Transformer. Whether code is released is also unknown.
- **Would make it more convincing:** Released code, and use by an independent team on a different model family.
- **Would prove it wrong:** Built-in relay-or-recombine networks that the test misclassifies.
- **What to do:** Read it this week. If the code is open, it's a candidate for a small methods grant to test it independently.
- **Compare with:** The adversarial collaborative review of integrated information and predictive processing theories, https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.00555

### 5. VAN challenged · **CRITIQUE**
- **Field / source:** Consciousness science · PsyArXiv preprint, 2026-11-18
- **Claim:** The Visual Awareness Negativity (VAN), a brain-wave signal about 200–300 ms after a stimulus that has been proposed as a specific marker of conscious perception, changes reliably with attention. That challenges whether it marks consciousness specifically.
- **Authors:** Łucja Doradzińska, Michał Bola (affiliations not in source).
- **Link:** https://osf.io/preprints/psyarxiv/ezn3p_v1
- **Evidence stage: 3.** Attention was manipulated experimentally, and Experiment 2 confirmed the effect. Whether the study was preregistered was not seen.
- **Impact if true:** MEDIUM. It weakens a widely used marker of consciousness.
- **Still unknown:** Sample sizes, effect sizes, and whether a VAN remains once attention is controlled.
- **Would prove it wrong:** A preregistered study where awareness and attention vary independently and the VAN tracks awareness only.
- **Who to follow:** Michał Bola.
- **What to do:** Read the full text before funding any VAN-based consciousness work.
- **Compare with:** The preregistered VAN study, https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5823961/

### 7. Dark energy limits · **BENCHMARK**
- **Field / source:** Dark energy and vacuum decay · arXiv preprint (astro-ph.CO), 2026-11-19
- **Claim:** Simons Observatory and CMB-S4 (next-generation telescopes mapping the cosmic microwave background, CMB) can't, on their own, tell DESI's preferred evolving dark energy apart from a standard cosmological-constant model that mimics the same CMB pattern. ΛCDM (the standard model) stays inside about 2σ.
- **Authors:** Anna Chiara Ferri, Ruchika, Alessandro Melchiorri (affiliations not in source).
- **Link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26305
- **Evidence stage: 0.** It's a forecast run on simulated data.
- **Why it matters:** This week's "dynamic vacuum energy" papers in physics.gen-ph (Millo 2611.23449; Chaudhary et al. 2611.23340) rest on the DESI DR2 dark energy hint. CMB data alone won't confirm it, so the evidence still depends on the DESI and supernova data.
- **What to do:** None. Keep it as the reference point for vacuum-decay claims.
